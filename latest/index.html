August took a heavy toll on the drumming world. Frank Beard, who held down ZZ Top's backbeat for more than fifty years, died on August 17. Kevin Feeney, who built Creative Percussion with his wife Cheri, died the same day. Earth, Wind & Fire drummer John Paris spent weeks in intensive care. We cover them because drummers are a small community, and we notice when someone leaves the room.

The month was not all dark. The Guitar Center Drum-Off came back after a decade and found a champion among more than 2,600 entrants. Zildjian opened its artist vault to Alesis and BFD. And Anika Nilles gave Rolling Stone Canada a candid look at what it is like to sit on Neil Peart's chair.

You are reading a rebuilt edition of September. The first version went out without photographs of its subjects, leaned on a few facts we had not checked hard enough, and missed the month's biggest story. We fixed it, and the corrections box lists exactly what changed.

Cover Story

01

The man with no beard, and the pocket that sold ten million records

Frank Beard's drumming was never flashy. It was the reason everything else in ZZ Top worked.

Frank Beard died on August 17 under hospice care at his ranch in Richmond, Texas. He was 77. ZZ Top announced the news on August 18. Guitarist Billy Gibbons called him “one of the most naturally innovative drummers” and said that “his signature backbeat was key to keeping ZZ on top.” Gibbons also said the band would keep touring, as Beard had wished, with bassist Elwood Francis and drummer Michael Monahan.

Beard was born June 11, 1949, in Frankston, Texas, and raised in Irving. He met bassist Dusty Hill as a teenager when their bands played the same Fort Worth club, and the two played together in a string of groups before ZZ Top. When the band's first drummer, Dan Mitchell, left in 1969, Beard took his place. He is also the one who introduced Gibbons to Hill.

The sound they built, Texas boogie and blues with a heavy swing, broke through with Tres Hombres (1973) and “La Grange.” “Tush” followed in 1975 as the band's first Top 20 hit. Billboard wrote this year that Beard's “explosive-but-swinging” playing was a huge part of the band's identity. Eliminator (1983), with “Gimme All Your Lovin',” “Legs” and “Sharp Dressed Man,” became the only ZZ Top album certified diamond by the RIAA, for more than ten million copies.

“His signature backbeat was key to keeping ZZ on top.”
Billy Gibbons, on Frank Beard · statement on Beard's death

Beard was open about his hardest years. In the late 1970s he spent heavily on drugs, went through rehab, and ZZ Top went on hiatus for three years. “I just wanted to get sober,” he later recalled. Engineer Terry Manning has said that Beard's condition affected his playing on Eliminator so badly that the drummer was dismissed from the sessions, and that Manning programmed drum machines and used samples for much of the album's percussion. Both things can be true: the record sold ten million copies, and Beard was the drummer who made the band worth seeing live.

Off the kit he was a character. He was known as “The Man With No Beard,” the only member of ZZ Top without one while Gibbons and Hill wore theirs to their chests, and he was credited as “Rube Beard” on the first two albums. In the early 1990s he raced sports cars at the 24 Hours of Daytona and the 12 Hours of Sebring, and the band cameoed in Back to the Future Part III. ZZ Top went into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2004.

His last years were quiet. He left the Elevation tour briefly in March 2025 because of illness, then returned. His final public show with ZZ Top was November 2, 2025, at Viejas Casino & Resort in Alpine, California. The band canceled its August 5 Hollywood Bowl date this year, reportedly because of his health. He is the second member of the classic lineup to die, after Dusty Hill in 2021.

Put on “Tush” and listen to nothing but the drums. The part is simple, it sits back, and it swings. That is harder than it sounds, and it is why the song still works.

This Month

02
Competition

The Guitar Center Drum-Off is back, and Jonathan Fortson wins it

The Drum-Off returned after nearly a decade with a national finale on August 20 at the Fonda Theatre in Hollywood, hosted by Bill Burr and Stephen Perkins. Six finalists had been drawn from more than 2,600 entrants. Jonathan Fortson of Hamilton, Alabama won.

Fortson takes home $25,000, a brand-endorsement opportunity, a pro acoustic kit with hardware and cymbals, a Roland electronic kit, and a trip to Drumeo to shoot original content. The other finalists were Chris “CJ” Darden, Jacob “Jake” Golden Ward, Mitchell “Mitch” Gorman, Caleb Robinson and Michael-Andrew Tao Cornish.

Sources (1)
  • Guitar Center press release, Aug. 24, 2026
Rush

Anika Nilles on sitting in Peart's chair, and four more dates

In Rolling Stone Canada's August 14 cover story on the Fifty Something tour, Anika Nilles, 43, described coaching herself in rehearsal not to get lost in the weight of the chair. The main thing she took from Neil Peart, she said, was how he composes a part so that it keeps building. She added that Peart's widow, Carrie Nuttall, sent her a message before the first show.

On August 31 the band added Houston (Toyota Center, October 1), St. Louis (Enterprise Center, October 21), Cincinnati (Heritage Bank Center, October 23) and Pittsburgh (PPG Paints Arena, November 15). Presales opened September 1 and general sales September 4. The tour was already booked through April 10, 2027.

Auction

Stephen Perkins opens his drum archive

Modern Drummer reported on August 21 that Jane's Addiction's Stephen Perkins would auction played kits, percussion and touring gear from his personal archive on September 10 through ArtistFirst on ANALOGr.com, with a portion of proceeds going to the Watts Conservatory of Music, where he is a board member.

Kits named in the story include a 1992 DW Porno for Pyros orange kit, a 1997 DW champagne sparkle short stack, a 2006 DW Jimmy Page custom, a 2008 DW Tribes exotic kit, a 2010 DW Jazz Series in titanium satin and a 2011 DW custom in black gunmetal sparkle.

Community

Two notes from the drum community

Earth, Wind & Fire drummer John Paris, 58, suffered a medical emergency before the band's August 6 show in San Francisco and was placed in an induced coma. By August 20 and 21, friends and family said he had woken and was stable, with two major surgeries and a longer ICU stay ahead. Sonny Emory filled in, and the San Francisco date was moved to September 26.

Kevin Feeney, who founded Creative Percussion with his wife Cheri in 2018, died on August 17 at 60 after colorectal cancer. Drumming News Network published the family notice on August 22.

Drummer Spotlight

03
Historical

Neil Peart, the Professor

September 12, 1952 to January 7, 2020. A new documentary about him premieres at TIFF on September 16.

Neil Peart was born in Hamilton, Ontario, and grew up in Port Dalhousie, now part of St. Catharines. After a discouraging stint in England he came home to concentrate on music and joined Rush, a Toronto band, in mid-1974. Rush's first tour with him began that August. He played on every Rush studio album from Fly by Night (1975) through Clockwork Angels (2012), and wrote most of the lyrics.

For all his reputation as a student of the instrument, the nickname “the Professor” actually came from his resemblance to a character on Gilligan's Island. The studying was real, though. He began as a hard-rock player drawing on Keith Moon, Ginger Baker and John Bonham, then moved toward jazz and big-band drumming inspired by Gene Krupa and Buddy Rich. In 1994 he became a friend and pupil of the jazz teacher Freddie Gruber and rebuilt his playing with jazz and swing in it. He also produced and played on two Buddy Rich tribute albums, Burning for Buddy (1994 and 1997).

At 30 he became the youngest person ever inducted into the Modern Drummer Readers Poll Hall of Fame. Rush went into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2013. Peart announced his retirement from touring in December 2015 after health problems, and he died of glioblastoma in Santa Monica on January 7, 2020, after a three-and-a-half-year illness.

Sampled cymbals

Zildjian opens its artist vault to Alesis and BFD

Announced August 6. Zildjian and Alesis Drums are putting Zildjian cymbal recordings into Alesis and BFD products, using cymbals from Zildjian's Artist Vault, which was historically reserved for endorsed artists. The first pack, Zildjian Session Mix Vol. 1, shipped the same day for BFD Player, BFD 3.5 and Alesis Strata modules.

DrumSpy listed its contents as 17- and 19-inch A Custom Rezo crashes, 15-inch A New Beat hi-hats and a 20-inch K Custom Dark Medium ride, plus a bubinga kit, at $39. More packs were scheduled through the rest of the year. This is sampled sound, not new metal.

  • Packs through year end

Pedals

Pearl Elimin8R bass drum pedals

Pearl introduced Elimin8R on August 13 to bring the best ideas from its Eliminator line down to a working drummer's budget: $139.99 for the P-1000CX single and $349.99 for the P-1002CX double. Both have a removable cam (progressive or linear action), a three-position PowerShifter, Click-Lock spring tension, a side-access hoop clamp, Uni-Lock beater angle, Control Core DuoBeaters and a sprocketless double-chain drive. The double adds an adjustable universal joint.

Pick of the Month

05
Drummer Pick of the Month

Ilan Rubin on Your Favorite Toy (Foo Fighters, 2026)

A new drummer, no click track, and a record made by two people in a room.

Your Favorite Toy came out April 24 on Roswell/RCA. It is Foo Fighters' twelfth studio album and the first with Ilan Rubin behind the kit. Dave Grohl and Rubin tracked the rhythm section live, without a click, and the rest of the band overdubbed on top.

Rubin spent 17 years with Nine Inch Nails, where the grooves had to be mechanical and lock to a click. Joining Foo Fighters was, by reports of the band's own comments, the first time in 17 years he had played without one. Grohl has said Rubin started out thinking everything had to be patterned and sound like the record, and that he encouraged him to loosen up.

That is our pick: not the flashiest playing of the month, but a real bet on feel over convenience. Put the record on, then listen for what a click track would have fixed. You will not find much, and that is the point.

Watch this month

Six drum streamers worth your Tuesday night

Six channels that play drums live, in the Twitch and Kick music categories. Follower counts were read from public stats pages on September 30, 2026 and will have moved since; schedules are quoted only where the streamer's own About page states one.

The8BitDrummer plays live to viewer song suggestions, leaning on video-game and internet music. About 458,000 followers.

Kriss_Drummer streams rock drum requests. His About page says he streams every Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, and lists a GEWA G5 as his streaming kit. About 207,000 followers. A 2025 Drumeo Awards nominee for YouTube Drummer of the Year (Drumming News Network).

66Samus streams drums on Twitch; his About page says he has been drumming for 30+ years and uploads to YouTube weekly. About 93,000 followers. He won YouTube Drummer of the Year at the 2025 Drumeo Awards (Drumeo).

Office Drummer (Kick) improvises live drums to viewer song requests, with priority requests and a request wheel. Its About page says the stream is for sharing music, not for stumping the drummer. About 4,100 followers (Kick About page). He played Kai Cenat's Mafiathon 3 in 2025 (VOD).

Kristina Schiano runs drum-request streams with subscriber-controlled lights. Her channel says it is live every Friday at 5pm ET. About 10,300 followers. A 2025 Drumeo Awards nominee for YouTube Drummer of the Year.

DangerDrums is a New York drummer and producer who takes live requests with flashing lights and releases his own electronic music. Per his YouTube channel, the rig is a Yamaha DTX750K with a Roland SPD-SX triggering Get Good Drums samples in Ableton Live. About 4,100 followers.

Our own channel, DrummerJawkneeD, streams on Twitch and Kick; check drummerjawkneed.com for the current schedule.
